Hammer of Kharas

Artifact

The Hammer of Kharas was named for the dwarven hero, Kharas, who wielded the hammer during the Dwarfgate Wars, although the hammer was forged more than two thousand years prior to that great battle. History prior to Kharas records it as being called the Hammer of Honor. Forged in 2,072 PC by the dwarves of Thorbardin as a replica of the hammer of Reorx, the dwarves presented it to Ergoth as an offering of peace. The hammer would remain with Ergoth for centuries, until it would appear once again during the Third Dragon War, when Huma Dragonbane sought out a dwarven smith in Ergoth, bearing the blessed Silver Arm of Ergoth and the Hammer of Honor. This dwarven smith, with the divine power of the Hammer of Kharas and the Silver Arm was able to forge the first dragonlances.

Effect: +1 Tactical Rating. +1 diplomacy with Kaolyn, Thorbardin and Zhakar.

Introduction

What is Dragons of Glory?

Dragons of Glory is a turn-based strategy game set in the world of Krynn, on the continent of Ansalon, during the War of the Lance. Modelled on the classic Dragonlance war game, it streamlines the tabletop rules: every country, army, fleet, dragon and hero is shown on a single hex map of Ansalon, and each campaign turn follows a fixed sequence of phases.

Two allegiances compete for control of the continent: the Whitestone Alliance of the free peoples and the armies of the Dragon Highlords. Neutral and minor powers join a side through diplomacy, conquest or special events.

  • The complete War of the Lance campaign covers the years 348–353 of the Age of Despair (turn 1 = March/April 348).
  • Strategic events are drawn every turn.
  • Each unit has a combat rating (CR), a movement allowance and, for leaders, a tactical rating (TR).

The Battle Turn

Turn sequence

Each battle turn follows this order. Player sides alternate in movement and combat.

  1. Initial Deployment - Happens only once. Each side deploys its starting units on the map.
  2. Replacements — Rebuild destroyed and depleted units and deploy replacements; the player without the initiative acts first. Draconian production also happens here.
  3. Strategic Events — event cards are drawn.
  4. Activation — Starting with the player without the initiative, each player can attempt to Activate a neutral nation of his choice by rolling 1d10.
  5. Initiative — each side rolls 1d4; the winner acts first. In case of a tie, the previous winner keeps the initiative.
  6. Movement & Combat — the first side moves and resolves its combats, then the second side does the same.
  7. Supply (Advanced only) — Units out of supply suffer attrition.
  8. End of turn — victory conditions are checked and the calendar advances.
First turn
Turn 1 is special: both deployment steps act as replacement steps and play jumps straight to Movement, skipping events, activation and initiative.

Initiative

In the initiative phase, it is decided which player acts first. The winner of the initiative roll may move and fight first, and also draws the first Strategic Event counter.

  • Classic: The winner is decided by a plain d4 roll. In case of a tie, the previous winner keeps the initiative.
  • Advanced: The player that does not have the initiative at the start of the game receives an initiative chit. The holder of the initiative chit may override the roll, handing the chit to the other player.
  • Disabled: Player order is fixed for the campaign/scenario (no dice are rolled).
Replacements

During the Replacement step, players can combine depleted units and deploy units from the reserve. The player without the initiative resolves their replacements first.

For every two armies of the same color combination (nationality) in their replacement pool, a player can immediately bring one of those armies back into play. The other army is destroyed.

In addition, if two depleted armies of the same nationality are stacked together in a hex during the Replacement Step, one of these armies can be placed in the Replacement Pool, and the other rebuilt to full strength. The army placed in the pool is immediately eligible to be used for additional replacements.

An army that is returned to play must be placed in any unoccupied city or fortress hex of its country.

Dragons, griffons and pegasi can never be replaced or rebuilt to full strength. If armies of these types are lost, they are permanently out of the game.

Countries & Diplomacy

The powers of Ansalon

The map contains 44 countries. Each one has a strength rating (the size of the forces it can field) and an alignment: a pair of modifiers telling how favourable it is to Whitestone and to the Highlord.

  • The Solamnic knight countries — Coastlund, Hinterlund, Nightlund, Solanthus, Southlund and Vingaard — receive a special Whitestone activation bonus.

Activation and allegiance

During the Activation Step of each Turn, each player may choose one neutral nation and attempt to Activate it as an ally by rolling 1d10. The player without the initiative rolls first.

Activation is successful if the roll is equal to or less than the nation's alignment rating for that side. The player may add any modifiers from events, leaders or artifacts to the roll.

Two countries cannot be turned by diplomacy alone: Dargaard and the gnomes of Mount Nevermind can only join a side through an event or by force.

Conquest

A country is conquered as soon as all fortified cities and fortresses in the country have been captured by enemy armies.

Armies are permanently removed from the game if their country is conquered.

Neutral invasion
The Highlord may invade neutral countries. The Whitestone side cannot invade neutral countries.

Units & Leaders

Unit types

Units are organised into four broad families: armies, fleets, dragons and leaders.

  • Armies — infantry and cavalry with a combat rating (CR) and a movement allowance.
  • Leaders — generals, admirals, wizards, emperors and Highlords provide tactical rating (TR) combat modifiers; eliminated leaders attempt to escape.
  • Dragons — wings of the ten dragonflights: five chromatic serve the Highlord, five metallic fly for Whitestone.
  • Fleets — warships that transport armies, control the seas and fight naval battles.
  • Wizards — support units that move freely across the map and cast magic.
  • Citadels — Magical floating citadels that can transport several armies and provide the same bonus as a fortified city.
Ratings

Combat rating (CR) is used to compute combat odds and to-hit rolls in naval combat. Tactical rating (TR) adds to the combat die roll when a leader is present in battle.

Every unit pays movement points to move; the default movement allowance is 5 points. Wizards move without spending points. Units with a terrain affinity ignore the movement cost of that terrain.

Unit statuses
  • Active — at full strength on the map.
  • Depleted — at reduced strength on the map.
  • Reserve — waiting in the replacement pool, off the map.
  • Destroyed — permanently lost (its equipment is gone and does not return to the pool).
  • Ready — available for deployment.
Stacking
No more than two units may occupy the same hex at any time.

Movement & Transport

Movement points and terrain

Ground units spend their movement allowance to enter hexes. In open terrain a hex costs 1 point; difficult terrain and bad borders cost more.

  • Ocean, desert, swamp, glacier and maelstrom hexes are impassable to ground troops.
  • Mountains are only entered through a pass or a tunnel border, and mountain combats can only be declared across a pass border.
  • River borders cost +1 movement point (they are free to cross when frozen in winter).

Combat

Land combat and odds

When a unit enters a hex containing an enemy force (or attacks across a pass border), combat is resolved. The attacker's total combat strength is compared to the defender's to produce a combat ratio (odds), expressed from 1:3 up to 6:1.

  • Odds beyond 6:1 are capped at 6:1; odds below 1:3 are treated as 1:3.
  • Walls, fortresses and bombards modify the ratio before the dice are rolled.
  • Leaders, armies, dragons and artefacts can each add a die-roll modifier (DRM).

Dragon duels and air supremacy

Before a land battle, each side's dragons fight an air-supremacy duel. Every dragon rolls one die per combat point and each roll of 4 or more scores a hit: hits step the target down ACTIVE → DEPLETED → DESTROYED.

The duel runs in rounds until one side is eliminated, both sides withdraw, or twenty rounds are fought. Players may withdraw their dragons between rounds; withdrawn wings relocate to a random hex.

Naval combat

Fleets fight in simultaneous rounds. Each operational fleet rolls a d10 and scores a hit on a roll equal to or below its attack rating. Hits are applied step by step: ACTIVE → DEPLETED → RESERVE, and a side may withdraw from the battle.

Sieges and Towers

Citadels and fortresses defend with a defence rating. A besieging army may bombard before assaulting; fortress modifiers shift the combat odds or the die roll in the defender's favour.

The Towers of Eli guard the sea approaches: each tower fires twice a turn, hits on a d10 roll of 4 or less and steps down fleet integrity ACTIVE → DEPLETED → RESERVE. Neutral towers fire on any invading fleet; once activated, an allegiance-controlled tower only fires against enemies.

Winter & Supply

Winter

Every five turns winter grips Ansalon — except inside the 'winter-free' region of the map (columns 40–64, rows 0–35), where normal movement is preserved.

  • Frozen rivers: river borders cost no extra movement, and deep rivers block fleet entry.
  • Snow-blocked passes: mountain passes become impassable to ground units.
  • Dragon wings halve their movement allowance (minimum 1).
  • Fleet survival: fleets in exposed waters roll a d6 each winter; a roll of 1 risks ice damage and forces a leader-escape check.
Supply

The supply system runs in Standard or Advanced mode, selected in the game options.

  • Standard: coastal hexes and ports are always in supply.
  • Advanced: units may additionally trace inland supply to the owning country or its capital.
  • Units that cannot trace supply suffer attrition (step losses) during the supply phase.

Naval Operations

Interception

Fleets that have not yet reacted may intercept enemy movement. In normal conditions an adjacent interception always succeeds (distance 1) and a distance-2 interception succeeds half the time (50%).

  • Winter restricts interceptions to distance 1 at 50%.
  • Interception rules can be toggled in the game options (Naval interception on / off).
Amphibious invasion

The Naval-invasion rules let a side land armies on enemy coasts through fleets. Landings are limited to coastal hexes and ports, and disembarking armies must respect the two-units-per-hex limit.

Strategic Events

How events work

Strategic events fire during the strategic-events phase of every fifth turn. Each event defines a trigger (or 'always'), a maximum number of occurrences, and an effect, and many belong to one allegiance.

  • Types: BONUS (diplomacy, combat or activation bonus), UNITS (reinforcements), BANNER (a country joins the cause) and ARTIFACT (a new artifact enters play).
  • Diplomacy events grant activation bonuses, e.g. Diplomatic Overture, Minor Alliance and Dark Alliance.
  • Artifact events put the war-winning relics into play, from the Dragonlance Forged to the Dragon Orb.

The full catalogue, with effects and pictures, is in the Events tab.

Victory

Victory conditions

Victory is measured with minor points: controlling countries, cities, fortresses and objectives grants points to a side. Each scenario defines its own victory conditions — the thresholds for a major or minor victory — and the game ends when a side reaches them at the end of a turn.

Single-country victories and fortress goals are common: reduce the enemy's headquarters or the Tower of the Dark Queen, hold a fixed set of objectives, or simply out-score the opponent when the calendar ends.
